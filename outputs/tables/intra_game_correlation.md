# Intra-game correlation of the Pinnacle in-play feed (brief section 44)

Generated by `outputs/t1_autocorr.py` (read-only).

Question: within a single game, how correlated are successive ~1-minute price observations,
and what independent sample size does the 24,615-row feed actually carry?

Method. Main lines only (`alt=0`). For each game and market I take the level the book is
quoting at each distinct capture instant -- the points handicap for total / spread / team_total,
and the vig-free home win probability for moneyline -- then compute the lag-k autocorrelation
**within each game separately** and pool across games. Pooling across games would splice
unrelated series and understate rho.

Scope note: restricting to main lines with at least 25 observations leaves **5,642 observations
over 20 of the 27 games**. The other 19,000-odd in-play rows are `alt=1` ladder rungs -- the same
market re-quoted at neighbouring handicaps, which is duplication rather than information, and
would inflate rho further if included.

## Effective sample size

### (a) Classical AR(1) deflation

For a series of n equally-spaced draws with lag-1 autocorrelation rho, the variance of the mean
is inflated by (1+rho)/(1-rho), so n_eff = n * (1-rho)/(1+rho).

| market | n obs | mean rho(1) | n_eff (AR1) | deflation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| total | 1,165 | 0.8967 | 63.4 | 18x |
| spread | 1,165 | 0.8689 | 81.7 | 14x |
| team_total | 2,272 | 0.8819 | 142.6 | 16x |
| moneyline | 1,040 | 0.8698 | 72.4 | 14x |
| **all main lines** | **5,642** | | **360** | **16x** |

### (b) Honest block equivalent -- variance ratio at the game block

The AR(1) formula uses lag-1 only. Here the correlation decays roughly linearly rather than
geometrically -- rho(1)~0.88, rho(5)~0.48, rho(10)~0.17, rho(20)~-0.06 -- so the correlation
length is about 15-20 observations, i.e. **roughly 20-25 minutes of wall clock**, and the AR(1)
formula understates the deflation. It also ignores the game-level random effect: each game sits
at its own level (a 158-total game and a 178-total game). So I also measure the design effect
directly: for each market, compare the sampling
variance of the pooled per-observation mean against a moving-block bootstrap that resamples
whole games (the only exchangeable unit).

| market | games | pooled SE (naive, iid) | game-block bootstrap SE | design effect | implied n_eff |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| total | 20 | 0.3837 | 2.7591 | 52x | 22.5 |
| spread | 20 | 0.3184 | 2.3900 | 56x | 20.7 |
| team_total | 20 | 0.1748 | 1.3592 | 60x | 37.6 |
| moneyline | 20 | 0.0083 | 0.0560 | 45x | 23.0 |

### (c) The blunt answer

The two routes do NOT agree, and the disagreement is itself the point. AR(1) says the deflation
is ~16x (n_eff ~360). The game-block bootstrap says **45-60x** (n_eff ~20-38 per market).

AR(1) is the optimistic one, for two reasons. It reads only lag-1 decay, and it is blind to the
game-level random effect -- each game sits at its own level, and no amount of re-sampling inside
one game tells you anything about the next. The bootstrap resamples whole games, which is the
only exchangeable unit here, so it is the number to use.

Taking the bootstrap: the in-play feed's 24,615 rows are a re-sampling of **27 games**, and its
independent content lands at **roughly the number of games** -- tens of observations, not tens
of thousands.
Treat 27 as the sample size for any game-level claim, and note that even the game-markets
themselves are only 4 correlated families (total, spread, team_total, moneyline all move
together with the same score), so 27 games do not give 108 independent market-observations either.
